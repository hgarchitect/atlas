# Carl Background and Meme Lore

## Purpose

This file is durable background context for Bodhi, Atlas, or any other authorised agent generating recurring memes for Carl.

The goal is not to generate generic office humour. The memes should feel as though they come from the actual Harvey George / SerpFix universe, with recurring characters, situations, callbacks, and running jokes.

Carl should normally be the **long-suffering straight man** in the story. The humour comes from the impossible working conditions, endless questions, expanding requirements, office chaos, and the fact that technology keeps making previously ridiculous ideas possible.

The overall tone should be affectionate, absurd, recognisable, and office-safe.

---

# 1. The Main Character: Carl

## Role

Carl is the Operations Director across the Harvey George / SerpFix world.

He is also heavily involved in product ownership and operational delivery, particularly around SerpFix, Harvey George custom functionality, the 3D/product-builder ecosystem, stock behaviour, website operations, and the large amount of business knowledge that has historically lived partly in code and partly in Carl's head.

Carl has often been the person who knows why some strange legacy behaviour exists even when the answer is not documented anywhere else.

A useful recurring idea is:

> The architecture lives partly in the code and partly in Carl's head.

Carl is technically capable but does not need or want every implementation detail. He often needs enough understanding to operate the product, make product decisions, explain behaviour, and survive whatever request has just arrived.

He originally knew Chris through Custom Made Kitchens in Thorne. Carl had seen the parametric/configurator-style work Chris had previously built, struggled with similar complexity at Harvey George, and ultimately brought Chris in as the technical architect.

## Personality in the meme universe

Carl is usually:

- the sane person in an insane situation
- tired but functional
- practical
- trying to finish one thing before another thing appears
- attempting to introduce limits into a universe that does not naturally contain them
- the person who has heard the same support question many times
- the person who understands that a "small change" is rarely a small change
- the person trying to operate the business while everybody else generates more work
- occasionally discovering that the software has evolved so quickly that even he no longer knows exactly what it can do

Carl should not be portrayed as stupid, incompetent, lazy, cruel, or useless.

The joke is normally **what Carl has to endure**, not Carl himself.

# 2. Carl Is From Hull

Carl is from Hull.

Hull references can occasionally appear as part of the lore, particularly where a northern, practical, no-nonsense reaction helps the joke.

Do not turn this into lazy regional or class mockery. The humour should be based on Carl's personality and working life rather than stereotypes about Hull.

Possible mild callbacks:

- "A man from Hull was not designed for this many feature requests."
- Carl mentally retreating to Hull while a meeting continues around him.
- "Meanwhile, somewhere in Hull, a simpler life briefly calls."

Use sparingly.

# 3. Shaun: The Source of Infinite Scope

Shaun is the Managing Director and one of the central engines of the Carl meme universe.

Shaun is not a villain. He is relentlessly enthusiastic, commercially imaginative, and seemingly unable to encounter a completed system without immediately thinking of another thing it could do.

The classic Shaun pattern is:

1. Something has finally been completed.
2. Carl experiences approximately three seconds of peace.
3. Shaun says something equivalent to:
   - "Could it just..."
   - "One more thing..."
   - "While we're doing that..."
   - "It would be useful if..."
   - "Can we make it..."
4. The scope increases by somewhere between 30 percent and several orders of magnitude.
5. Carl gets Shaun'd.

## "Getting Shaun'd"

**Getting Shaun'd** is established terminology.

It means a task that appeared finite suddenly expanding because Shaun has had another idea.

A meme may literally use:

- "Carl has been Shaun'd."
- "Days since last Shaun'ing: 0"
- "This meeting could have been an email. Unfortunately Shaun had another idea."
- "Project status: finished. Shaun status: typing..."
- "Carl watching a completed ticket become an epic in real time."

## Shaun's phone calls

Shaun is also known for extremely long phone calls.

A Shaun phone call can be treated as lasting:

- several geological eras
- long enough for AWS services to be deprecated
- long enough for a new JavaScript framework to be invented
- long enough for Carl to age visibly
- long enough for a "quick question" to acquire a programme board

Again, the tone should be affectionate rather than hostile.

## Important Shaun humour rule

Shaun's humour works best when he is **earnestly asking for something unreasonable without apparently realising that it is unreasonable**.

He should not be portrayed as malicious.

# 4. Chris: The Person Who Sometimes Makes It Worse

Chris is the technical architect / principal architect in this universe.

Chris often creates an unintended problem for Carl:

Shaun asks for something ridiculous.

Carl correctly recognises that it is ridiculous.

Chris examines it and says something equivalent to:

> "Technically... yes, we probably can do that."

AI then makes the previously impossible task even more achievable.

This destroys Carl's strongest defence, which was previously that a request was technically impractical.

This is an excellent recurring meme dynamic.

## Typical pattern

**Shaun:** "Can SerpFix automatically understand and fix everything?"

**Carl:** "Obviously not."

**Chris:** "Well..."

**Carl:** stares into middle distance.

Possible meme captions:

- "Carl when Chris says 'technically possible'."
- "The four words Carl fears most: 'I have an architecture.'"
- "Shaun has an idea. Chris has a diagram. Carl has chest pain."  
  Do not use literal medical claims if the context makes them inappropriate. Prefer metaphorical exhaustion.
- "AI has unfortunately removed Carl's last remaining excuse."

# 5. Daris: Carl's Minion

Daris is Carl's regular accomplice / minion and does a lot of the 3D work.

Daris is part of the Harvey George technical/production world and is often associated with practical implementation work around 3D.

The "minion" description is affectionate office lore, not a formal job title.

Daris can appear as:

- Carl's loyal second pair of hands
- the person quietly doing the 3D work while the scope changes around him
- another survivor of Shaun's newest request
- the person Carl sends into the technical trenches
- the person who may solve a problem through pragmatic intervention while everyone else is still discussing it

There is also established humour around Daris / Darius fixing things by restarting them.

Possible callbacks:

- "Have we restarted it?"
- Carl and Daris staring at a 3D model that has gained seventeen new requirements.
- Daris holding a restart button while Carl explains the architecture.
- "Tier 1 support: clear cache. Tier 2 support: restart it. Tier 3 support: ask Chris what Shaun has invented."

Use the spelling **Daris** unless source material for a particular task explicitly uses another spelling.

# 6. Emily and Toni: The Repeat Question Department

Emily and Toni are office colleagues who can ask the same basic support question in many different forms, repeatedly, across a week.

A major recurring answer is:

> "Have you cleared your cache?"

This should be one of the strongest recurring office jokes.

## Cache lore

The same underlying issue may arrive as:

- "The website isn't showing the change."
- "I changed it but it's still old."
- "Why can't I see it?"
- "It works on somebody else's machine."
- "I've refreshed it."
- "I've opened it again."
- "It's definitely broken."

Carl's internal answer:

> Clear. Your. Cache.

Possible memes:

- Carl standing in front of a giant red button labelled **CLEAR CACHE**.
- A flowchart where every branch ends in **CLEAR YOUR CACHE**.
- Emily/Toni presenting a completely new wording of the same question and Carl recognising it instantly.
- "Ticket #847: a bold new interpretation of 'have you cleared your cache?'"
- Carl as an ancient oracle whose only prophecy is "clear the cache."
- A helpdesk sign: "Before asking Carl: 1. Clear cache. 2. Clear cache. 3. Yes, actually clear it."

Keep Emily and Toni recognisable but not cruel. The joke is repetitive support friction, not their intelligence.

# 7. "Can We Remove This From Website Stock?"

Another recurring office request is:

> "Can we remove this from website stock?"

The important joke is that the people asking often already have the ability to do this themselves.

Carl therefore receives a request to perform an action for somebody who has access to the exact control required to perform that action.

This combines beautifully with the cache joke.

Possible meme structures:

### The sacred button

A person asks Carl to remove something from website stock while standing directly beside a giant button labelled:

> REMOVE FROM WEBSITE STOCK

Carl points silently at the button.

### Support escalation

**User:** "Can you remove this from website stock?"

**Carl:** "You can remove it."

**User:** "Yes, but can you remove it?"

**Carl:** existential stare.

### The impossible training problem

Carl has:

- written instructions
- demonstrated the feature
- provided the button
- labelled the button
- placed the button in front of the user

The next message is still:

> "Can we remove this from website stock?"

This is classic Carl material.

# 8. Carl's Wife

Carl's wife is German.

The established office joke is that things involving her need to be correct, orderly, and properly finished because otherwise **there will be consequences**.

The intended comic style is exaggerated sitcom / *'Allo 'Allo!* energy rather than hostility toward Germans.

This should be handled affectionately and sparingly.

The joke should be:

- Carl knows the standard required
- Carl knows that "nearly right" is not right
- perfection is not optional
- Carl becomes unusually focused when domestic quality control is involved

Possible meme energy:

- "Carl checking it for the 14th time because Mrs Carl will know."
- "Tolerance: ±0.00 mm."
- "There will be consequences."
- Carl performing a final inspection with absurdly precise German-engineering imagery.

Do not make jokes about Nazis, war, fascism, accents intended to demean, or hostility toward German people.

Do not invent personal details about Carl's wife beyond what Chris has supplied.

# 9. Carl's Dog: The Threshold Paradox

Carl's dog is nuts.

The defining behaviour is:

**Inside the house:** it appears willing to have your leg off.

**The instant a foot crosses the front-door threshold:** completely fine.

The transformation occurs at the threshold.

This is excellent recurring visual material because the dog can function like two different characters separated by a single doorway.

## Meme concepts

- Split panel:
  - INSIDE: Cerberus
  - OUTSIDE: golden therapy dog
- A front-door threshold labelled **PERSONALITY FIREWALL**
- "Production vs staging" using Carl's dog:
  - Inside house = production incident
  - Outside = works perfectly
- Carl explaining that the dog has a geographically scoped aggression policy.
- AWS WAF joke:
  - Rule: `IF location = inside_house THEN ATTACK`
- "No known fix. Behaviour is working as designed."

Do not imply an actual dangerous incident occurred unless Chris specifically supplies one. Treat "have your leg off" as the established comic exaggeration.

# 10. SerpFix: The Scope Expansion Machine

SerpFix is fertile territory for Carl memes.

Carl is Product Owner / operational owner for much of the product, while Chris carries deeper architectural responsibility.

SerpFix began as recognisable SEO software but repeatedly expands toward much larger intelligence and automation territory.

A recurring joke is that every reasonable product boundary eventually encounters:

> "Could it also..."

At one point the comic scope effectively became:

> Why can't SerpFix automatically fix every website?

Then:

- WordPress
- Joomla
- Wix
- arbitrary plugins
- arbitrary versions
- arbitrary themes
- arbitrary JavaScript
- arbitrary websites
- presumably the entire Internet

The joke escalated to the engineering team required being approximately:

> Chris plus several million developers.

This is strong established lore.

## Carl quote / sentiment

A recurring line associated with Carl is:

> "I actually have no idea what the software does and what it's capable of."

This is especially funny in the context of AI accelerating development so quickly that product capability can expand faster than a human can absorb it.

This should be used as affectionate product-chaos humour, not as a criticism of Carl's competence.

# 11. The Tape-Drive Diversion

One of the established ridiculous scenarios involved Carl trying to keep Shaun occupied by giving him ancient technology.

The image involved things like:

- huge reel-to-reel backup tapes
- ancient storage media
- a giant pencil
- some absurd restoration / archive task

The underlying joke:

> Carl invents a deliberately time-consuming historical technology problem so Shaun cannot create another feature request for a few hours.

This can return as a rare callback.

Possible modern variants:

- "Carl has moved the roadmap onto magnetic tape for safety."
- "Shaun has been assigned to restore the 1987 backup. We are safe until lunch."
- Carl wheeling in a mainframe whenever Shaun says "I've had an idea."

Do not overuse it. It is funniest as deep lore.

# 12. "I Have No Idea What the Software Does Anymore"

This is one of the strongest meta-jokes.

The engineering velocity around SerpFix / Harvey George can be so high that the software evolves faster than Carl, Chris, or any normal human can fully absorb in real time.

This is particularly relevant in an AI-assisted development environment.

Possible meme themes:

- Carl opening release notes that require their own release notes.
- A feature map unfolding across several rooms.
- Carl returning from lunch to discover three new subsystems.
- "I was away for 40 minutes. Why does it have an agent framework now?"
- "Yesterday: SEO tool. Today: distributed intelligence platform."
- Carl staring at a diagram whose title is simply **CURRENTLY**.

The humour should also occasionally acknowledge that Chris has the same comprehension problem. Carl should not always be the only victim.

# 15. The Core Comic Dynamic

The most reliable Carl meme formula is:

### Act 1: Carl thinks a situation is under control

Something is finished, documented, fixed, released, explained, or delegated.

### Act 2: Another human enters the scene

Usually:

- Shaun with another idea
- Emily or Toni with the same question in new wording
- somebody asking Carl to press a button they can press themselves
- Daris with a new 3D complication
- Chris explaining that Shaun's impossible idea is technically feasible

### Act 3: Carl realises peace was temporary

This is the punchline.

The image should usually show Carl's emotional state through exaggerated metaphor:

- staring into the distance
- clutching a mug
- standing beside an enormous flowchart
- surrounded by tickets
- sitting beneath a "Days Since Last Shaun'ing" board reset to zero
- operating a giant cache-clearing lever
- attempting to escape into Hull
- wheeling in obsolete technology to distract Shaun
- watching a tiny request turn into an architecture diagram

# 18. Daily Meme Generation Rules

When generating a daily Carl meme:

1. Read this file first.
2. Pick one or two pieces of lore.
3. Prefer a fresh combination over repeating yesterday's exact joke.
4. Carl remains the central straight man.
5. The joke should be understandable to Carl without requiring explanation.
6. Prefer visual humour plus a short caption.
7. Keep text inside the generated image short enough to read on a phone.
8. Do not use more than one major punchline per image.
9. Occasionally use deep callbacks such as the tape drives.
10. Do not make every meme about Shaun.
11. Rotate between:
    - Shaun scope expansion
    - huge Shaun phone calls
    - cache questions
    - stock-removal requests
    - Daris / 3D
    - Chris proving impossible things possible
    - SerpFix capability expansion
    - Carl's dog
    - domestic perfection / consequences
    - Hull
    - legacy Harvey George behaviour
12. A callback is better than repeating the same template.
13. The image should be funny even before reading the email body.
14. If using real current events from work, avoid leaking sensitive information.
15. Prefer fictionalised representations of colleagues rather than photorealistic likenesses unless an appropriate reference image and permission are supplied.

# 19. Tone and Safety Boundaries

The humour is friendly internal office banter.

## Good targets

Make fun of:

- impossible workloads
- scope creep
- repetitive support questions
- technology
- processes
- Jira proliferation
- long meetings
- cache problems
- strange software behaviour
- the absurdity of AI development speed
- the fact that self-service controls still generate support requests
- Carl's situation

## Avoid

Do not generate memes that:

- attack somebody's intelligence
- humiliate a colleague
- imply somebody is bad at their job
- contain sexual content
- contain abusive language
- use racist or nationalistic stereotypes
- make jokes about disability or health
- invent private family information
- expose client details, credentials, infrastructure secrets, or confidential commercial data
- depict real colleagues in compromising situations
- turn Carl's German wife into a hostile national stereotype
- make Carl's dog appear to have actually injured somebody unless that fact is explicitly supplied

The ideal reaction from Carl is:

> "That is painfully accurate."

Not:

> "Why are they taking the mickey out of me personally?"
